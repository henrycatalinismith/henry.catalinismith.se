After more than [ten happy years with my hand-built keyboard](/2024/08/18/ergodox-ten-years-later/), when my trackpad battery began to die recently I was keen to replace it with something similar. The trackpad had also been causing a minor RSI for a while, so I was interested in finding a replacement with better ergonomics. A little bit of googling led me to the [Ploopy Adept](https://ploopy.co/adept-trackball/). Open source trackball, some assembly required, wired connection, no battery to worry about. Exactly what I’m looking for! Sold!

<figure>
 <img
  alt="A profile view of a trackball mouse with a black case and a red ball."
  src="/2026/10/07/IMG_0531.webp"
 />
</figure>

The [assembly instructions](https://github.com/ploopyco/adept-trackball/wiki/Ploopy-Adept-Trackball-Kit-Assembly) for the Adept are wonderful. They include all the info you need without wasting any words.

Unlike the original Ergodox, the Adept’s PCB ships 99% ready to use. No difficult surface-mount diodes to solder this time, which is probably for the best. Not sure I have that kind of free time these days. You only have to solder one chip onto the PCB.

<figure>
 <img
  alt="A blue soldering mat with a PCB, a microchip, and a soldering iron."
  src="/2026/10/07/IMG_6805.webp"
 />
</figure>

I hadn’t soldered anything since assembling the Ergodox back in 2014. It took a moment to get the hang of it again. Had forgotten how weirdly exciting the smell of fresh solder is. It smells… I dunno… "important"? Or something?.

Anyway, the first time I plugged in my Adept, the buttons all worked, but the trackball didn’t. The assembly instructions have a section for this exact case.

> The sensor likely isn't soldered correctly. Double-check all of your solder connections, touch them up with some flux if you have it, and try again.

Dead right. Look at the _state_ of these joints. No wonder the poor thing didn’t work yet.

<figure>
 <img
  alt="Close-up of some solder joints on a PCB. One or two of the joints are visibly not soldered well, with solder only on the leg of the chip."
  src="/2026/10/07/IMG_0527.webp"
 />
</figure>

After a few minutes more of poking it with the soldering iron, I reassembled it and it was alive.

This thing is gorgeous!

<figure>
 <img
  alt="A side view of a trackball mouse with a black case and a red ball."
  src="/2026/10/07/IMG_0532.webp"
 />
</figure>

It’s a joy to use, too. Trackballs are underrated as hell. And compared to the switch to the Ergodox, which took months to fully adjust to, this was nothing. Very happy!
